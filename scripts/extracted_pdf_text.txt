1. Executive Summary: Why Standard Stats Mislead
In T20 cricket, raw career averages and economy rates treat every run and wicket equally. A batter
scoring 65 off 40 balls on a flat pitch in a one-sided state-league fixture looks identical on paper to a
batter scoring 65 off 40 balls to rescue a team from 22/4 in a T20 International or an IPL Playoff
chase. Similarly, a bowler conceding 7.50 runs per over on a slow 130-run pitch appears superior to a
death bowler conceding 7.80 runs per over in a 215-run shootout.
To solve the Round 1 challenge, we built the Hierarchical Leverage-Weighted Pressure Rating
System (HL-PRS v5). Written to be intuitive for coaches, scouts, and analysts alike, our system
answers four core questions for every player:
How tough was the situation? Did the player perform during a top-order collapse, a steep 175+ or
200+ run chase, a low-total defense, a razor-thin final-over thriller, or a tournament knockout match?
How strong was the competition? Performing in a T20 World Cup or the IPL carries higher weight
than performing in domestic state leagues. *(Strictly T20 matches are used in the math; U19 ODI
World Cup and List-A feats are noted only as qualitative background).*
Did the player rise or choke in knockouts? How does their pressure output compare to their normal
baseline, and do they consistently deliver—or repeatedly fail—in elimination playoff games?
How many times have they proven it? Players who deliver across a larger sample of high-pressure
matches earn a controlled longevity bonus over small-sample cameos.
Forensic Data Cleaning: Three Critical Database Bugs Fixed Before Modeling
Before calculating any ratings, we audited all 109,733 rows of player_match_stats.csv and
5,086 rows of matches.csv and corrected three major upstream data errors that would otherwise
distort player rankings:
1. The Super Over N× Multiplication Bug (169 Rows / 16 Matches): In tied matches decided by a
Super Over, every player who batted in the Super Over had their regulation row duplicated 2 or 3
times—and their regulation runs, balls, fours, and sixes were multiplied by 2× or 3× inside each
row. For example, in Match 28962 (India vs Afghanistan, 17/01/2024), Rinku Singh appeared 3
times with 207 runs off 117 balls in each row (his true 69* off 39 × 3), and Rohit Sharma appeared
twice with 242 runs off 138 balls (his true 121* off 69 × 2). We divided by the duplicate count and
deduplicated to restore exact real-world scorecards.

2. The Base-10 Fractional Over & 4.1+ Over Bug (848 Rows): The pre-computed economy_rate

stored as 'Quarter Final ' (with a trailing space) vs 18 as 'Quarter Final'. Stripping
whitespace recovered all 60 quarter-finals.

Our rating system is built in four simple steps so anyone can follow and reproduce a player's score:

Step 1: Match Leverage Weight (Li) — Not All Pressure Matches Are Equal
Each high-pressure match receives a multiplier Li that combines the quality of the tournament, the
importance of the round, and how severe the in-match crisis was:
Match Leverage (Li) = Competition Weight (Wcomp) × Stage Multiplier (Mstage) ×
Crisis Multiplier (Msituation)
Strict Competition Hierarchy (Wcomp): T20 World Cup / Asia Cup (1.35) > Bilateral T20
Internationals (1.25) > IPL (1.15) > India A Emerging/Rising Stars T20 (1.00) > Major Overseas
Leagues [SA20, BBL, CPL, PSL, Blast, MLC] (0.95) > Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy Elite (0.82) > State
T20 Leagues [TNPL, Maharaja, UP T20, DPL, MPL, KCL] (0.70).
Knockout Stage Multiplier (Mstage): Final (1.50×) > Semi-Final / Qualifier / Eliminator (1.35×) >
Quarter-Final (1.25×) > Super 4 (1.10×) > League Match (1.00×).
Crisis Situation Multiplier (Msituation): Starts at 1.00 and adds +0.55 for a Severe Collapse (or +0.35
Standard Collapse), +0.55 for a Winning 200+ Chase or Winning Low-Total Defense (or +0.30 for 175+
Chase / +0.35 Shootout), +0.45 for an Ultra-Close ≤5-run / ≤2-wicket / Super Over thriller (or +0.25
Close Margin), and +0.20 for winning Player of the Match.
Step 2: Pitch-Adjusted Baselines & Bayesian Small-Sample Smoothing
To ensure a bowler at a high-scoring venue (like Bengaluru or Delhi) is compared fairly against a
bowler on a slow turner (like Chennai or Mirpur), we compare every player against the Match Par
Strike Rate and Match Par Economy Rate of that specific game (ΔSRpar and ΔEconpar). We also
measure how much a player elevates their game under pressure compared to their normal career
baseline (ΔAvg = Pressure Avg − Usual Avg and ΔSR = Pressure SR − Usual SR). To prevent a
player with 2 lucky innings from outranking a 50-match veteran, we apply Empirical Bayes
Smoothing, blending each player's leverage-weighted stats with the T20 role average (150-ball prior
damper).
Step 3: The Habitual Knockout Flop Deduction (−Pflop)
A player cannot be called "reliable under pressure" if they dominate league games but repeatedly fail
in knockout playoffs. If a top-7 batter gets out for ≤15 runs (with 1.25× extra severity for single-digit
<10 dismissals) or a bowler concedes ≥9.50 RPO with ≤1 wicket in at least 45% of their true
elimination knockouts (minimum 3 knockout games), our formula deducts up to −14.00 points.
Players who deliver match-winning knockout heroics receive 0.00 penalty.
Step 4: The Pressure Sample-Size & Longevity Bonus (+Bvol)
To give a fair advantage to players who have delivered in high-pressure situations dozens of times
over those with only a handful of games, we add a logarithmic volume bonus based on total pressure
appearances (Npress) and high-impact pressure performances (Nimpact):

Volume Bonus (Bvol) = 1.35 × ln(1 + Npress / 8) + 0.65 × ln(1 + Nimpact / 5)
Final HL-PRS Score = Base Pressure Score − Knockout Flop Penalty (Pflop) + Big-
Stage Bonus + Volume Bonus (Bvol)

9. Reliability, Sensitivity Checks & Model Limitations
Sensitivity Across Weighting Changes: Testing four different weight setups (Baseline HL-PRS v5,
2× Knockout Weight, 2× Sample-Size Bonus, and Fixed Unadjusted Bayesian Shrinkage) yields a 0.94
to 0.96 Spearman Rank Correlation. Our Top 10 Batters and Top 10 Bowlers remain stable across
every sensitivity test.
Match-Level vs. Delivery-Level Granularity: Because player_match_stats.csv records full-match
player totals rather than ball-by-ball timestamps, our system reconstructs top-order collapses
(top3_cheap_outs) and match par rates at the innings level. It cannot directly separate balls 1–10 of a
batter's innings from balls 25–40, or split a bowler's overs 1–6 Powerplay economy from overs 17–20
Death economy within the same 4-over spell.
Non-Boundary Strike Rotation Proxy: Since batting dot balls faced are not listed in the match log,
we track strike rotation under pressure using Non-Boundary Strike Rate: (Runs − Boundary
Runs) / (Balls − Boundary Balls) × 100. Elite pressure anchors like Tilak Varma (81.4), Sai
Sudharsan (79.8), and Salman Nizar (78.6) rotate strike on nearly 4 of every 5 non-boundary balls,
preventing dot-ball pressure from building.
